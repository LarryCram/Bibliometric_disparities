\title[Assessment-Bound Disparities]{Assessment-Bound Disparities: Divergence Between National Evaluation Submissions and Index-Based Bibliometrics in Australia's ERA 2018}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Lawrence} \sur{Cram}}\email{lawrence.cram@anu.edu.au}

\affil*[1]{\orgdiv{College of Science}, \orgname{The Australian National University}, \orgaddress{\city{Canberra}, \postcode{2601}, \state{ACT}, \country{Australia}}}

\abstract{
National research evaluation exercises routinely compile massive, curated corpora of scholarly outputs to assess institutional performance. In Australia's Excellence in Research for Australia (ERA) 2018, the 40 eligible Higher Education Providers (HEPs) submitted 567,647 outputs, reconciling into 455,741 distinct canonical works published between 2011 and 2016. A pervasive assumption in evaluative bibliometrics is that an assessment-defined corpus mirrors the scholarly record indexed under institutional affiliation addresses. Linking ERA submissions against OpenAlex, we find deep, structural divergences between the two corpora. Across 2011--2016, OpenAlex records 744,574 works bearing an Australian HEP author affiliation, of which only 401,261 (53.9\%) appear in ERA; 343,313 works (46.1\%) were not submitted. While 75,348 unsubmitted works represent document types formally excluded by assessment rules (preprints, meeting abstracts, book reviews, editorials), over 222,000 are standard peer-reviewed journal articles, books, and book chapters. Crucially, 45,822 of these unsubmitted Australian works are directly cited by ERA papers (generating 109,443 citation links), including landmark methodological contributions recording thousands of citations. Furthermore, among submitted works, the institution claiming the paper in ERA matches the published institutional address on the work in only 67.6\% of cases, with 13.8\% (49,962 works) bearing zero Australian institutional affiliation. This institutional attribution divergence exhibits a strict monotonic temporal gradient across the six-year retrospective window, with purely foreign affiliations rising from 8.4\% in 2016 to 19.4\% in 2011. Grounded in Olensky's framework of bibliometric data accuracy, we demonstrate that these disparities arise from the structural divergence between two fundamentally different bibliometric constructs: evaluating the retrospective output of an institutional workforce fixed at a single census date versus measuring the scholarly output produced within an institutional address.
}
